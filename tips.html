Getting started with CamLog

If you've used a camera log app before, the data entry will feel familiar. Start with Install and Offline Sync.

1. Install it before your first day

CamLog is a web app, not an App Store download. It runs in a browser tab, but functions best when it is installed as an app on your home screen.

Add it to your home screen

iPhone / iPad: open app.camlog.app in Safari, tap the Share button , then tap Add to Home Screen. Chrome and Firefox on iOS can't do this. It has to be Safari.

Android: open app.camlog.app in Chrome, tap the menu (⋮), then tap Add to Home Screen or Install app.

Desktop: Chrome or Edge show an install icon in the address bar.

Why installing matters

An installed CamLog launches full screen with no address bar, and is far better protected from the browser housekeeping described below. Phone browsers clear the local data of sites you only visit occasionally; an installed app is treated as an app, not as just a stale tab open in a browser.

On iPhone and iPad it goes further: the installed app gets storage of its own, separate from Safari's. That's why you sign in again after installing, and why clearing Safari leaves it alone. Deleting the home-screen icon deletes that storage with it, so don't remove and re-add the icon to fix something. Sync first if you do.

Sign in once while you have signal

Creating your account and the very first load need a connection. After that, CamLog opens and works with no service at all. Do this at the hotel, not at the location.

There are no updates to install

New versions are automatically pushed to your phone whenever you have connection. When a new version has arrived you'll see a small banner. Tap it, or tap the Synced pill, and the app reloads onto the new version, no connection required. The version number is at the bottom of the projects screen if you ever need to report a bug.

2. Offline is the normal case, not the emergency

CamLog was built for stages with no bars. Everything you type is saved locally before it's synced to the cloud.

Every keystroke saves to the phone first

There is no Save button, and there is no spinner to wait out. What you type is written to the device immediately, and a background process pushes it to the server whenever a connection exists. Losing signal mid-take changes nothing about how the app behaves.

Read the sync pill

The small pill at the bottom of the screen tells you exactly where your work stands:

  • Synced: everything on this device is also on the server. Tap it to refresh the page and pick up any new app version.
  • Syncing: changes are on their way up right now.
  • Offline: no connection. Keep logging; it's all saved locally and goes up on its own.

Give it a minute of signal before you wrap

The one habit worth building: at the end of the day, once you're back in coverage, open CamLog and wait for the pill to read Synced. That's your confirmation the day is fully uploaded to the server.

Don't sign out with work still pending

Signing out clears the local copy on that device. If anything hasn't synced yet, CamLog will stop and tell you how many changes are at risk. Read that dialog rather than tapping through it. Wait for the Synced pill, then sign out.

3. About clearing your browser

Short version: your synced work is safe. Only unsynced work is at risk.

Everything you log goes up to the server as soon as you have a connection, free projects and Pro projects alike. When your phone is offline the data you enter is saved locally. When browser data is cleared, you are deleting that locally saved data. Once signed back in, all project data saved to the cloud returns, but data that was only saved locally will not.

Two helpful habits:

  1. Install CamLog to your home screen. This gets rid of automatic cache clearing and treats CamLog data as belonging to a dedicated app, not just a stale tab.
  2. Never clear browsing data or delete the app while you're out of signal. Check that the pill says Synced first. That's the whole safety check. You'll find it bottom-left on your productions and cameras lists; it's hidden while you're logging to keep the screen clear. On Android in particular, treat “delete browsing data” as deleting CamLog from that phone.

4. A few hidden gestures

Some actions are gestures rather than buttons to keep the log screen clear.

Deleting takes: the trash button

Above the take list sit three small buttons: a pencil, a circle, and a trash can. The trash can switches the list into delete mode: every take gets an × on the right, and the fields go read-only. Tap an ×, confirm, and that take is deleted. Tap the pencil to go back to editing.

Or swipe a take to the left

If you'd rather not switch modes, drag a take row to the left and the same Delete confirmation screen appears. It's a shortcut for people who like gestures.

Tap the × to delete a scene

Every scene card has an × in its bottom right corner. Tap it, confirm, and the scene is removed. You then get a few seconds to Undo before it's committed. A deleted scene can be restored in Recovery mode.

Hold a scene to move it

Press and hold anywhere on a scene card for about half a second and it lifts. The grid icon in the top right corner grabs it immediately with no wait. Tapping the card opens the scene.

Swipe down to dismiss a sheet

Any panel that slides up from the bottom can be swiped back down, or dismissed by tapping outside it. On Android, the back gesture does the same thing without leaving the app.

5. Nothing is really deleted: Recovery mode

This is the single most reassuring thing to know about CamLog.

Where it is

The archive icon in the header of the Productions, Cameras, and Scene lists toggles Recovery mode. Turn it on and everything you've deleted from that list appears alongside what's live, ready to restore. Turn it off and you're back to the working view.

Deleting is reversible, and it stays that way

A deleted production, camera, or scene isn't erased. It's set aside. There's no countdown and no automatic purge, so a scene you removed on day one is still restorable on day forty. Recovery mode works offline too: the archived copies live on your device as well.

Hide vs. Delete on a shared production

Hide Production removes a show from your list only. It's the right move for a job that wrapped and is cluttering your screen. It's also reversible from Recovery mode.

Leave Production is different: it gives up your access, and you'd need a fresh invite to get back in.

A camera must be empty before it can go

You can't delete a camera that still has active scenes on it. Delete or move the scenes first. It's a guardrail against wiping a day's work with one tap, and the camera stays restorable afterwards either way.

6. Logging faster

The parts that pay off once you're moving at set speed.

Smart Fill, twice

The wand button copies from your last scene in two stages. The first tap fills the settings that rarely change between setups: stop, ISO, fps, shutter, color temp, filters, format. Tap it again and it fills everything else, scene number included. It only ever writes into empty fields, and an Undo appears for a few seconds after each tap.

Circle mode

Toggle the take list into circle mode and each take becomes a single button: one tap to circle, one tap to un-circle. It's built for calling circle takes at the end of a setup, without the risk of editing anything else on the screen.

Film cameras get a dial column

On a camera marked as film, each take row gains a footage-dial field, and CamLog shows the difference from the previous take next to it. Log the raw dial reading and let it do the subtraction.

Two takes with the same number is a flag, not a bug

If a duplicate take number appears highlighted, it usually means two people edited the same scene while one was offline and both entries survived. CamLog flags it rather than silently merging, because only the person holding the camera report should decide which is right.

Opening an old scene warns you first

Come back to a scene that's been untouched for a while and CamLog asks before letting you type. It's there to stop the classic mistake of logging a new setup into yesterday's scene.

Search and roll grouping

Long scene lists have a search that can be narrowed to a specific field, and scenes group under their roll so a day reads the way the camera report does.

7. Working with a crew

Single-user projects are free and unlimited. Live multi-user sync is the one thing that costs anything: $8 once, per project, paid by whoever set it up.

Invite by link or QR code

The admin generates an invite from the team menu and shares it as a link or holds up a QR code for people to scan. Nobody else pays. Your 2nd ACs, loader, and script supervisor join free, on their own accounts. You can cap how many people a single invite lets in, and revoke it at any time.

Editors and viewers

Invite someone as an editor and they can log; invite them as a viewer and they get a live read-only window on the day, the right setting for a producer or a post supervisor who should see the log but never change it. Roles can be changed later, and the admin can be handed to someone else.

Edits appear live, and recent ones are marked

On a shared project, changes from other people show up as they happen. A scene someone else touched recently is marked so you know at a glance that it moved.

Set your display name

Do this when you join. Without it your crew sees an email address in the team list instead of a name.

8. Exporting your data

Three formats, different purposes.

PDF camera report

The one you send to production and post. Export a whole camera, or use the custom export to pick specific days, cameras, or rolls when you only need part of the show.

CSV for post

A spreadsheet of the log, for editorial or for anyone who wants the data rather than the document.

JSON backup

A complete copy of the production that you can re-import later, hand to another crew member, or just keep on a drive.

CamLog Wrapped

Visualizing your show's data

At the end of a show, send a camera's log straight to Wrapped for a set of shareable stats about the job: lenses, stops, take counts. Purely for fun, and a good way to explain the app, whether it's to the rest of the crew or your friends.
